INTERPRETATION
Early rejection stops cipher work after the first crib mismatch. It saves most
work when the crib starts immediately; a late crib still requires every prefix
keypress. The one-letter case offers no transformation reduction at all.
The optional implementation calls key_press for each letter, which normalizes and
checks each character; baseline.process validates each whole candidate message
and loops internally. Those different costs are part of the measured API calls.
Per-character overhead plausibly offsets modest work
savings in short/late cases. This was not a profiler experiment and does not
attribute a measured fraction of runtime to validation, allocation or CPU effects.

Both 97-letter cases preserve two valid starts, UEW and VFW. In the implemented model,
with rotors I-II-III, UEW has the middle rotor at its E notch, so its pre-key step
moves left and middle as well as right, giving VFX. VFW moves only right, also to
VFX. The two states coincide before the first signal under these fixed settings.
Therefore a longer crib cannot distinguish those starts in this model; two
answers are not evidence of failed search. The one-letter case has 714 matches.
